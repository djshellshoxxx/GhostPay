# GhostPay compliance research (informational)

**NOT LEGAL ADVICE.** Informational research by an AI agent for the project owner. Not reviewed by a lawyer. Retrieval date for all sources: **2026-10-21** (today). Primary government sites (fincen.gov, treasury.gov, eur-lex.europa.eu, courts) were **unreachable from the research sandbox** (proxy blocked), so most facts rest on secondary sources (law-firm notes, trade press, advocacy groups). Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** or **[MEMORY]** (agent background knowledge, not fetched) must be checked against primary text before reliance. Owner jurisdiction is assumed US but **unconfirmed**.

## 0. Bottom line (tentative)

1. Biggest risk driver is **control + fee + knowledge**, not code publication. Immutable, non-upgradeable contracts with no admin key and no owner-run gatekeeping sit in the best-supported position (Van Loon; FinCEN 2019 software language; DOJ Aug 2025 statement; BRCA if enacted).
2. A **0.25% fee per routed payment sent to the owner's wallet** is the feature most likely to look like "business" revenue from transmission. The Storm and Samourai prosecutions both leaned on the developers earning fees from a service. Whether a router fee turns a software author into a "money transmitter" is **an open legal question** (see section 5 and lawyer questions).
3. The **hosted parts** (relayer, frontend, smart-account service) are where the owner is an operator, can screen, and can be asked to. Treat those as the regulated-ish surface: sanctions screening, ToS, geoblocking, logs.
4. US federal law is **unsettled**: Storm is convicted on one count (not yet sentenced; Rule 29 pending; retrial Apr 2027); CLARITY/BRCA failed cloture in Sept 2026.

## 1. United States

### 1.1 FinCEN FIN-2019-G001 (May 9, 2019)
- Guidance applies existing BSA MSB rules to convertible virtual currency (CVC) models; fact-and-circumstances test, labels do not control. Source (secondary): https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/financial-services-law/fincen-provides-guidance-on-cryptocurrency (retrieved 2026-10-21). Primary PDF: https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf (appeared in search, **not fetched**).
- Framework **[MEMORY, verify in primary]**: *user* (obtains CVC to buy goods/services; not an MSB), *exchanger* and *administrator* (engage as a business in accepting and transmitting value; money transmitters), plus sections on hosted wallets, multi-sig, DApps, kiosks, payment processors, and "anonymizing software providers." Memory of the key lines: (a) a person that creates/sells *software* that merely lets users transmit is not itself a transmitter on that basis if it does not itself accept and transmit value; "anonymizing software providers" (suppliers of tools) are not money transmitters, whereas "anonymizing *services* providers" that accept and transmit value are; (b) unhosted wallet providers that never accept/transmit are not MSBs; (c) DApp owners/operators who use the DApp to accept and transmit value may be transmitters. Coin Center's reading that the guidance matches non-custodial/software distinction: https://coincenter.org/fincens-new-cryptocurrency-guidance-matches-coin-center-recommendations/ (retrieved 2026-10-21).
- Mapping to GhostPay (preliminary, for lawyer): router contract = software/DApp; relayer = operator that submits transactions and may take a fee; question is whether relayer "accepts and transmits value" (relayer typically never holds user principal, but it fronts gas and is repaid, which a regulator might analyse as value flow). **[UNCERTAIN]**
- 2026 FinCEN: no 2026 FinCEN guidance on non-custodial software was found; rulemaking found is GENIUS Act stablecoin-issuer AML/sanctions NPRM (FinCEN/OFAC, Apr 2026), which targets issuers, not routers. Sources: https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-04/PPSI-AMLCFT-NPRM.pdf ; https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2026-june/treasurys-proposed-stablecoin-compliance-framework/ (retrieved 2026-10-21; search snippets only).

### 1.2 DOJ position (Aug 2025) and criminal statute 18 U.S.C. 1960
- Aug 2025, Matthew Galeotti (DOJ Criminal Division) said writing code without ill intent is not a crime; DOJ would not approve **new** charges under 1960(b)(1)(C) against a third party for truly decentralized software that only automates peer-to-peer transactions where the party lacks custody/control of user assets; intent-based charges (money laundering, sanctions evasion, aiding and abetting) remain possible; 1960(b)(1)(A)/(B) (licensing/registration) need willfulness. Sources: https://www.theblock.co/post/367858/writing-code-without-ill-intent-is-not-a-crime-says-us-doj-official-following-tornado-cash-verdict ; https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/sitecore/content/whlaunch/home/research/news-publications/20250910-doj-signals-approach-to-digital-assets-what-it-means-for-developers-and-platforms ; https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/key-points-from-doj-new-defi-outline (all retrieved 2026-10-21; speech text itself not fetched). It is a policy statement, **not law**, binds no future administration or private/state litigants.
- Coin Center criticism that DOJ's stance still threatens wallet developers: https://coincenter.org/dojs-new-stance-on-crypto-wallets-is-a-threat-to-liberty-and-the-rule-of-law/ (retrieved 2026-10-21; advocacy).

### 1.3 Legislation: Blockchain Regulatory Certainty Act (BRCA) / CLARITY Act
- BRCA (Emmer H.R. 1747; Lummis/Wyden S. 3611 per secondary source) would exclude "non-controlling" blockchain developers/service providers (no legal right or unilateral ability to initiate or effect user transactions) from 31 U.S.C. 5330 and 18 U.S.C. 1960. It was folded into the CLARITY Act (Sec. 604 per sources). Sources: https://www.defieducationfund.org/myths-vs-facts-the-blockchain-regulatory-certainty-act/ (advocacy) ; https://hodder.law/clarity-act-defi-developer-safe-harbors-stablecoin-regulation-2026/ ; https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/05/28/clarity-act-shields-crypto-developers-but-one-criminal-line-could-gut-it/ (retrieved 2026-10-21). Senators Grassley and Durbin reportedly objected over the 1960 change **[secondary, UNVERIFIED]**.
- Status: CLARITY passed the House July 2025, cleared Senate Banking May 2026, **Senate cloture failed 49-50 on Sept 15, 2026**; motion to reconsider entered; many expect no market-structure law before 2027. Sources: https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/09/senate-fails-to-advance-the-clarity-act ; https://defirate.com/clarity-act-fact-sheet/ (retrieved 2026-10-21). **Not law. Do not rely on it.** Even if enacted, "non-controlling" would likely not cover an operator who runs a relayer and takes fees **[owner analysis, lawyer to confirm]**.
- GENIUS Act (stablecoin law, July 2025): regulates payment stablecoin issuers; relevance to GhostPay is indirect (if USDC-type tokens are routed, issuer freeze/blocklist capability exists). **[MEMORY for enactment date; verify]**

### 1.4 OFAC / sanctions
- Tornado Cash designated Aug 8, 2022 **[MEMORY]**. *Van Loon v. Treasury*, 5th Cir., Nov 26, 2024: immutable smart contracts are not "property" of a foreign national/entity under IEEPA, so OFAC could not block them; relied on Loper Bright. Source: https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/12/federal-appeals-court-tosses-ofac-sanctions-on-tornado-cash-and-limits-federal-governments-ability-to-police-crypto-transactions ; https://www.gtlaw.com/de/insights/2024/12/fifth-circuit-rejects-ofac-sanction-of-tornado-cash-crypto-code (retrieved 2026-10-21). Binding only in the 5th Circuit; leaves other issues open.
- **March 21, 2025**: OFAC removed Tornado Cash from the SDN list, citing discretion in light of the Van Loon filing. Sources: https://thedefiant.io/u-s-treasury-removes-sanctions-against-tornado-cash-cryptocurrency-mixer-978cfd78 ; https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/export-controls-trade-investment-sanctions/1603170/the-tornado-cash-delisting-and-sanctions-compliance-implications-for-crypto (retrieved 2026-10-21). Treasury press release not fetched. Delisting did **not** concede that smart contracts can never be sanctioned, and OFAC still can designate persons/addresses it can show are owned/controlled **[inference]**.
- Implication: **immutable router** (no owner, no upgrade, no fee switch controllable by a person) is hardest to treat as blockable "property." An **operator-run relayer/frontend** is a person/service that can screen, so a US person operating it is subject to OFAC strict-liability civil exposure for dealing with SDN-linked parties **[MEMORY: civil liability is strict; verify]**. Note a fee flowing to the owner's wallet makes the owner a counterparty to every payment, including sanctioned senders, if the router is not screened.
- OFAC list data: OFAC Sanctions List Service (SLS) provides SDN/Consolidated downloads and an API, free: https://ofac.treasury.gov/other-ofac-sanctions-lists ; https://sanctionslist.ofac.treas.gov/Home/static/index.html (retrieved 2026-10-21; hostname details per vendor pages, verify).

### 1.5 State money transmitter licensing
- US states license "money transmission" separately (about 49 states plus DC; NY BitLicense for virtual currency business). Many states define transmission as receiving money/value for transmission; some use "control" tests; the Conference of State Bank Supervisors Model Money Transmission Modernization Act (MTMA) adopted by many states **[MEMORY; no source fetched; UNVERIFIED]**. Not researched in depth (research gap). Lawyer should confirm whether a relayer/sponsor fee or smart-account service triggers any state regime, particularly NY and CA (DFAL, effective 2026 **[MEMORY, verify]**).

### 1.6 Tax on fee revenue (high level)
- Fees received in crypto are generally ordinary income at fair market value when received (IRS Notice 2014-21 / Rev. Rul. 2023-14 principle, **[MEMORY]**); self-employment tax may apply if carrying on a trade or business; later disposal gives capital gain/loss; entity choice matters. Not researched with sources this pass. Ask a CPA/tax lawyer. Foreign entity does not avoid US tax if the owner is a US person (CFC/GILTI rules) **[MEMORY]**.

## 2. EU / UK

- **MiCA** (Reg. (EU) 2023/1114): Recital 22 says services provided in a "fully decentralised manner without any intermediary" fall outside scope. Source (quoted via AMF France): https://www.amf-france.org/sites/institutionnel/files/pdf/73017/en/The_European_regulation_Markets_in_Crypto-Assets_%28MiCA%29.pdf (retrieved 2026-10-21; commentary notes article numbering is inconsistent across sources, check OJ). Commentary says the test is functional: an identifiable intermediary taking fees or holding upgrade keys probably needs a CASP licence; admin keys, centralised frontends and DAO governance are grey areas; ESMA technical clarity expected 2026 **[secondary, UNVERIFIED]**: https://www.bitsofblocks.io/post/in-the-eye-of-the-needle-decentralisation-under-mica ; https://ryder.id/blogs/post/mica-crypto-regulation-what-europes-rules-mean-for-self-custody (retrieved 2026-10-21). Self-custody wallet software by itself reportedly does not trigger CASP authorisation; the line is control of keys.
- **AMLR** (Reg. (EU) 2024/1624), **Art. 79**: credit/financial institutions and CASPs may not keep anonymous accounts, or accounts allowing anonymisation, or accounts using anonymity-enhancing coins; applies **from July 2027** (exact day, commonly cited 10 July 2027, not verified). Sources: https://cointelegraph.com/news/eu-crypto-ban-anonymous-privacy-tokens-2027 ; https://coingeek.com/eu-law-banning-anonymous-digital-asset-wallets-by-2027-final/ (retrieved 2026-10-21). Effect on GhostPay: not a ban on the software as such (as read), but EU CASPs/exchanges may refuse to handle stealth-address flows; EU users of a hosted relayer face CASP-side friction. How Art. 79 applies to self-hosted wallets/non-custodial tools was **not resolved** by sources.
- **Travel Rule** (Reg. (EU) 2023/1113): applies to CASPs, not to software authors **[MEMORY]**.
- Dutch precedent: Alexey Pertsev (Tornado Cash) convicted of money laundering in the Netherlands, 64 months (May 2024); appeal pending, outcome not found. Source: https://www.theblock.co/post/339401/tornado-cash-developer-alexey-pertsev-gets-nod-for-conditional-release-from-prison-to-work-on-appeal (retrieved 2026-10-21; **appeal outcome UNVERIFIED**).
- **UK / other**: not researched this pass (research gap). UK MLRs 2017 crypto-asset exchange/custodian wallet providers regime and FCA financial promotions rule are the starting points **[MEMORY; verify]**.

## 4. Practical controls for the HOSTED parts only

(Hosted = relayer, public frontend, smart-account/paymaster service, any API the owner operates.)

1. **Sanctions screening of relayer requests.** Screen sender, recipient/stealth address, and token before relaying/sponsoring; fail closed with logged reason code.
   - OFAC SDN/Consolidated lists, free via SLS download/API: https://ofac.treasury.gov/other-ofac-sanctions-lists (retrieved 2026-10-21). Digital currency addresses appear as identifiers on SDN entries. Poll daily, hash/version each list pull.
   - Chainalysis free sanctions oracle (on-chain contract) and free API (key) launched Mar 2022: https://decrypt.co/94808 ; https://cointelegraph.com/news/chainalysis-launches-free-sanctions-screening-tools/ (retrieved 2026-10-21). The contract address `0x40C57923924B5c5c5455c48D93317139ADDaC8fb` is from agent memory and was **NOT verified** against Chainalysis docs; confirm on docs.chainalysis.com and block explorer per chain; coverage is sanctions-list only (not risk scoring) and per-chain deployment varies.
   - Paid risk-scoring options (TRM Labs, Elliptic, Chainalysis KYT): pricing is quote-based; **no public pricing verified** — treat as UNKNOWN.
   - Limitation: a stealth recipient address is fresh and unscreenable in advance; screen the sender, the sponsoring user, funding source and the announcement's ephemeral metadata where feasible, and document the limitation.
2. **Geoblocking** the OFAC comprehensively sanctioned jurisdictions at frontend/API (IP + user attestations); IP blocking is imperfect; log blocks. Jurisdiction list: OFAC program list, verify at https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-programs-and-country-information (not fetched).
3. **Terms of Service / disclaimers**: non-custodial statement, user responsibility for lawful use, sanctions/ prohibited-use representation, no warranty, right to refuse relay, privacy notice, arbitration/limitation clauses as lawyer advises. ToS does not shield from criminal/OFAC exposure.
4. **KYC for non-custodial software**: no source found that requires KYC of users by a non-custodial software author or by a pure software relayer; the premise rests on FinCEN's software/non-MSB reading (section 1.1) and DOJ's statement (section 1.2). If the owner ever is an MSB, KYC/AML program, SAR filing and registration apply. **Treat "no KYC needed" as lawyer-confirm, not settled.**
5. **Record keeping**: retain relay request logs (hashed/minimised for privacy), screening results, list versions, blocks, abuse reports, and law-enforcement requests; define retention (BSA records are 5 years if MSB **[MEMORY]**) and a response process for subpoenas/OFAC inquiries. Privacy policy must match what is logged.
6. **Kill switches and gating** on hosted components only (allowlist/denylist, pause of sponsorship) — noting that any admin power over the router itself weakens the "immutable" argument (see matrix).

## 5. Decision matrix (what moves risk)

Legend: lower / higher relative regulatory exposure; judgement is the agent's inference unless sourced. **Not legal advice.**

| Choice | Lower-risk option | Higher-risk option | Reasoning / sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fee on router | Voluntary tip, or fee set to zero in immutable router and monetise only hosted add-ons | Mandatory 0.25% per-payment fee to owner wallet | Fee income from every transmission resembles transmission-as-business; 1960 cases involved fee-earning services (section 3). BRCA "non-controlling" and DOJ "no custody/control" tests do not mention fees but a fee stream correlates with "operating a business" **[inference]**. A fee address controlled by a single key is also an owner-control surface. Alternative: fee to an immutable/DAO-style sink (still needs lawyer). |
| Router mutability | Immutable, no owner/upgrade/pause | Upgradeable proxy or admin pause | Van Loon turned on immutability (https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/12/federal-appeals-court-tosses-ofac-sanctions-on-tornado-cash-and-limits-federal-governments-ability-to-police-crypto-transactions). MiCA: upgrade keys suggest an intermediary (secondary sources, section 2). |
| Relayer operation | Publish relayer software; let third parties run relayers, no owner fee skim | Owner operates the only relayer and takes % fee | Operator is a person that can screen and be reached by OFAC/FinCEN; revenue plus operation fits "business" in FinCEN fact-and-circumstances test (section 1.1) **[inference]**. |
| Hosting frontend | Only publish code/IPFS builds, no owner-operated UI | Owner hosts and brands a frontend | Hosted UI = identifiable service, geoblock-able, subpoena-able; Treasury and EU commentary treat frontends as grey area (section 2, CLARITY open DeFi front-end issue per DLA Piper link above). Publishing code alone best aligns with DOJ Aug 2025 statement. |
| Smart-account flat fee | Charge via hosted service with ToS, screening | Bundle into protocol-level mandatory fee | A fee for an owner-run service is plainly commercial; keep it separate from the router. |
| Entity location | Take advice; entity must match actual control | "Offshore entity" while owner remains US person | US jurisdiction follows US persons and USD/US nexus; offshore does not remove OFAC exposure for a US-person owner and has tax implications (section 1.6). EU entity brings MiCA/AMLR questions (section 2). |
| Marketing | Neutral "privacy for payments" positioning, abuse policy | Marketing around evading enforcement | Prosecutors cited marketing to criminals in Samourai (https://decrypt.co/347236/us-prosecutors-seek-maximum-sentence-for-samourai-wallet-developers). |

## 6. Questions for a lawyer

1. Is the owner a US person; which state/country; what entity structure is best, and does it change federal/state exposure?
2. Does a mandatory 0.25% router fee payable to the owner make the owner a "money transmitter" under FinCEN guidance, 31 U.S.C. 5330 or 18 U.S.C. 1960, even with immutable contracts and no custody? Does a voluntary tip or a fee to an immutable sink change the answer?
3. Does operating a gas-sponsorship relayer (fronting gas, repaid in-kind or in token with a percent fee) constitute accepting and transmitting value? Is a registered MSB / state licence needed?
4. Does the flat smart-account fee raise any separate issue (e.g., account abstraction bundler/paymaster service)?
5. OFAC: what is the minimum defensible screening programme for a US-person relayer operator? Is screening senders sufficient given fresh stealth recipients? Should a voluntary self-disclosure policy exist?
6. How does Storm (one conviction, Rule 29 pending, retrial Apr 2027) and Samourai affect risk for a project with a router fee? Should launch wait for the Rule 29 ruling or the 2027 retrial?
7. Is it safe to publish and promote code, run only a frontend, or neither? Where is the line between publishing and "operating"?
8. Terms of service, privacy policy, abuse policy, DMCA/law-enforcement-request process: lawyer-reviewed drafts needed.
9. EU/UK: does offering the hosted relayer or frontend to EU/UK users trigger MiCA CASP or UK MLR registration? Impact of AMLR Art. 79 (July 2027) on EU users.
10. Tax: character and timing of fee income, self-employment tax, entity choice, token fees, and recordkeeping.
11. Is there any benefit to seeking a FinCEN administrative ruling or no-action style comfort, or a legal opinion letter before launch?
12. Insurance, personal liability shielding, and developer anonymity risks.
