GhostPay

Privacy and local data

Stealth addresses reduce address reuse. They do not hide the public transaction graph, and they do not make your device, browser, wallet, provider or host trustworthy.

In brief: the blockchain is public, your RPC provider sees your queries, and the sweep destination can link a payment to your wallet. GhostPay keeps seller keys in your browser and has no analytics. It does not promise anonymity or automatic refunds, and it has not been audited.

What remains public

Public-chain transactions reveal the payer, asset, amounts, timing and contract interactions. Announcements carry the data used to discover stealth payments. Moving funds from a one-time address can connect it to the sweep destination. The payer already knows the address they paid.

A seller's public meta-address is a stable identifier. Reusing it across invoices can connect those invoices. Publishing it in a public registry connects it to the publishing account. Public configuration is not private-key material, but it can still link devices and public identities.

GhostPay's app code has no analytics, telemetry, third-party scripts or remote fonts. These statements describe the app code. They do not describe what your host, network, browser or extensions record.

What stays in this browser

  • Settings, including RPC endpoint URLs, relayer URL and pinned signer keys, are stored without encryption in localStorage. A credential inside an endpoint path or query is visible in the browser profile.
  • Seller private keys stay in memory while the key set is loaded. Saving the optional browser vault stores only the keystore ciphertext in IndexedDB. An exported keystore is a separate encrypted file.
  • Tags and reconciliation records are encrypted in IndexedDB. Some record existence and size may remain visible to anyone who can inspect the browser profile. Unlocked content is readable by the running page.
  • Wallet and sweep attempt records keep transaction references and unresolved outcomes. They are not key backups, and clearing them cannot cancel an on-chain action.
  • Imported historical observations and stored receipts do not gain independent payment verification merely by being stored or restored.

Locking a view restricts its current access. It does not erase stored records or downloaded files. Records remain until you delete them or clear the site's storage. Export the backups you need before clearing site data, and delete exported copies separately when you no longer need them.

Links, QR codes and backups

Clearing the address bar reduces exposure after an invoice has been read. It cannot remove the link from browser history, copied text, screenshots, device backups or extensions. Use the app's paste workflow when you do not want to open a link directly.

Preserve original receipt and backup files. A QR code is a representation, and its image can be incomplete or lose context. Public configuration cannot restore private keys. Keep the encrypted keystore and its passphrase separately from every other archive.

Privacy options have limits

The scan transport list includes a Tor Browser label. The app does not route its requests through Tor, so the label does not hide your address from the provider. Selecting a label does not create an onion service, install Tor or hide public blockchain activity.

Experimental policy accounts and the external privacy-pool fixtures have separate acceptance gates. They are not a promise of anonymity, and they are not an operational recovery service in the ordinary invoice flow.

What this page does not claim

  • It does not claim anonymity or unlinkability. Stealth addresses reduce address reuse only.
  • It does not claim an independent audit. The source repository's audit records are the place to check status.
  • It does not claim automatic refunds. Refund execution is unsupported in the app.
  • It does not claim that your host, network, wallet, extensions or chosen providers keep no records.

Keep evidence claims separate

A valid invoice asks for payment. A seller receipt signature attests to its signed fields and key control. An observation reports data. Verified payment evidence requires the relevant checks against the correct chain, transaction, recipient, asset, amount and finality. None of these alone establishes the human identity of a seller.
